01THE IDEAMORE THAN A CV

Talent is more than
a list of experience.

Think You Can Build is a live problem-solving show and graduate talent showcase.

Three to five graduates take on the same computer-based business case in one facilitated call — each making their own attempt. You’ll have room to ask questions, test ideas, explain your choices, and show how you respond when new information lands.

Your facilitator works like a senior colleague or project lead: they set up the case, ask everyone the same questions, and help make your thinking visible. It’s one more way to show your skills to the people who might be looking for them — alongside your CV, not instead of it.

05HOW YOU’RE SCOREDA PUBLISHED RUBRIC

NO GUESSWORK

Six things.
One clear scale.

Each criterion is scored 1–5, with a written example of what the reviewer saw. You’ll see the rubric before you sign up. A score describes your work on this task — not your worth or your potential.

01

Problem understanding

1
Misses the need or constraints.
3
Finds the main need, asks useful questions.
5
Frames the underlying need and revises with new facts.
02

Reasoning

1
A conclusion with little explanation.
3
Plausible approach, checks an assumption.
5
Compares options and explains why one fits.
03

Feasibility

1
Ignores practical constraints.
3
Workable next step, names a limitation.
5
Shows the path, dependencies, and trade-offs.
04

Communication

1
Hard to follow.
3
Explains the approach, responds adequately.
5
Clear structure, honest about uncertainty.
05

Collaboration

1
Doesn’t engage constructively.
3
Listens and contributes respectfully.
5
Makes room for others, disagrees well.
06

Learning from feedback

1
Doesn’t consider new information.
3
Adjusts reasonably after a prompt.
5
Seeks feedback and explains what changed.

HOW A WINNER IS CHOSEN

  1. 01

    The facilitator scores each candidate, citing one example per criterion.

  2. 02

    A second reviewer independently scores the strongest submissions.

  3. 03

    Reviewers compare evidence and record why a shortlist or winner was picked.

  4. 04

    Shortlisted graduates review their solutions with the facilitator — for coaching, not scoring.

  5. 05

    You get feedback and can correct factual errors in your record.

✳ Viewer comments are moderated and never count toward the score.

06THE FINE PRINT, UPFRONTREWARDS & FAIR PLAY

STRAIGHT TALK

What’s on the table.
How we keep it fair.

We’d rather under-promise. Here’s what winners can expect, what depends on a company saying yes, and the ground rules every challenge runs on.

WHAT YOU COULD GET

Planned for winners
  • ✳Interview & presentation grooming
  • ✳A CV review / building session
Only if a company agrees
  • ↗A company interview or business pitch
  • ↗Job shadowing or a short internship
  • ↗A paid role
  • ↗A cash bounty

We’ll only advertise rewards that are confirmed before a challenge goes live.

FAIR PLAY, BY DESIGN

  • You see the terms first. Brief, rubric, stream conditions, recording and reuse terms, and rewards — before you register.
  • Same brief, same time, same prompts. No one gets extra hints.
  • Two reviewers, one rubric. Individual evidence notes, so airtime and confidence don’t stand in for ability.
  • Comments don’t count. They’re moderated, and popularity is never part of the score.
  • Mock companies first. No real company is named without written permission.
  • Only what we need. Minimal personal data, kept separate from anything public.
  • You can pause or stop. If you ask, if private info shows on screen, or if there’s a safety concern.
!

Heads up: challenges are planned to be streamed publicly. A private or recorded-only option is still an open decision. Reasonable accommodations will be offered where we can.

11GOOD QUESTIONSFAQ

ASK AWAY

Before you
jump in.

The questions graduates are most likely to ask — including the ones we’re still working out.

Is this a guaranteed job or internship?

No. A challenge adds evidence of how you work — it doesn’t replace interviews or other hiring checks. Interviews, shadowing, internships, paid roles, and bounties only happen if a company agrees to them.

Do I have to appear on camera?

Public visibility is central to the idea, so challenges are planned to be streamed. A private or recorded-only option is still being considered. You’ll see the stream, recording, and reuse terms before you agree to anything.

Am I competing against the others in the call?

Everyone makes their own attempt at the same case in a shared call. You’re scored individually against the rubric — not on how confident or talkative someone else was.

Do viewer comments or likes affect the result?

No. Comments are moderated and never count toward your score. Views and likes measure attention, not ability.

How long is a session?

Roughly 90–120 minutes for three to five graduates. That’s a draft estimate — it will be tested in a dry run first.

What field will the first challenge be in?

It isn’t chosen yet. It will be a computer-based challenge picked after speaking with graduates and employers, and it shouldn’t need expensive software or equipment.

Who owns my solution?

Plain-language terms on solution use, attribution, and reuse will be published before each challenge. First challenges use fictional mock companies.

What happens to my personal information?

Only what’s needed is collected, contact details are kept separate from anything public, and access and deletion rules are set before any data is collected. This prototype doesn’t send or store anything.
