# Think You Can Build — Pitch Deck Copy V1

## Slide 1 — Think You Can Build

**Graduate Employability**

**Turn problem-solving into visible proof.**

A live challenge show where graduates tackle business problems on camera, helping employers see how they think beyond the CV.

## Slide 2 — The experience gap

**Capable graduates can be hard to assess before their first job.**

Employers often have limited evidence of how an early-career candidate reasons, collaborates, and responds to feedback.

In South Africa, graduate unemployment was **12.4%** in Q2 2026, compared with **33.6% overall**. This is context for the challenge, not proof of its cause.

*Source: Statistics South Africa, QLFS Q2 2026.*

## Slide 3 — A cycle with few chances to prove yourself

**No experience can mean fewer opportunities to gain experience.**

Graduates need a first chance to build work history. Employers need credible signals before hiring. CVs and short interviews may not show how someone approaches a real task.

**Affected:** early-career graduates with limited formal experience.  
**Decision-makers:** employers and hiring managers.  
**Working model — needs validation:** founder observations and project framing; no stakeholder interviews yet.

## Slide 4 — The root is an evidence gap

**Hiring teams need to make decisions with incomplete signals.**

Formal job history is visible. Practical reasoning, learning, and collaboration are harder to see in a CV.

**Working hypotheses — unvalidated:** limited assessment time, reliance on familiar hiring signals, and uneven access to networks can reinforce the gap.

The project has not yet confirmed these causes through graduate or employer research.

## Slide 5 — Make the work visible

**Show the process, not just the polished answer.**

Think You Can Build gives employers a chance to observe graduates clarify a problem, test an approach, explain trade-offs, and adapt to feedback.

**Design hypothesis — to test:** a structured, role-relevant work sample can reveal useful evidence that a CV or short interview may miss.

## Slide 6 — One challenge. Several ways to show capability.

**Graduates solve the same case. Reviewers score the work.**

Three to five graduates make individual attempts in one facilitated call. A facilitator introduces a mock-company business case and prompts participants to show their thinking.

The facilitator and a second reviewer use a shared rubric. Viewers can watch; comments do not determine the winner.

**Planned MVP winner benefits:** interview and presentation grooming, plus a CV review/building session. Delivery capacity still needs confirmation.

## Slide 7 — Prototype / product demonstration

**The product is at concept stage.**

**NOT BUILT YET:** no working app, clickable prototype, or live challenge exists today.

**Proposed demo flow:** challenge brief → graduate registration → facilitated challenge stream → scored evidence profile → replay gallery.

The first version is planned around existing call and streaming tools, with a lightweight website/dashboard.

## Slide 8 — Evidence so far

**The need has context. The concept still needs testing.**

- **Official context:** Stats SA reported 12.4% graduate unemployment in Q2 2026.
- **Founder observation:** peers from the founder’s college cohorts have struggled to work in their field. This is anecdotal and not representative research.
- **No validation yet:** no graduate survey, employer interviews, prototype test, pilot, or confirmed company partner.

**Next evidence:** survey graduates and interview employers before selecting the first field.

## Slide 9 — Start small with existing tools

**Test the format before building the full platform.**

**Proposed stack:** React + Node.js + Express + MongoDB (MERN).  
**Proposed delivery:** Teams or Meet for calls; OBS or StreamYard for production; YouTube for public video.

Run one mock-case pilot manually, with a facilitator, second reviewer, and moderator. Build the full dashboard only after the workflow proves useful.

**Feasibility status — untested:** solo founder today; no app, budget, team, or platform workflow confirmed.

## Slide 10 — Exposure needs safeguards

**Public visibility can create opportunity—and risk.**

- Public streaming may exclude graduates who cannot or do not want to appear on camera.
- Consent does not erase the risks of a recording being copied or seen later.
- Live confidence, airtime, internet quality, and comments can distort perceptions.
- Real business cases can expose confidential information or raise ownership questions.

**Proposed safeguards:** clear consent and reuse terms; mock cases first; moderated comments; equal prompts; individual evidence notes; independent scoring. A private or recorded participation option remains an open design decision.

## Slide 11 — Measure evidence and opportunity

**Success means employers learn something useful—and graduates get a fair chance to show it.**

**Pilot measures:** completion and withdrawal; participant fairness feedback; reviewer score agreement; employer usefulness; follow-up requests; confirmed interviews, shadowing, internships, or paid roles.

Views and likes measure attention, not hiring impact. No impact results exist yet.

## Slide 12 — Prove the format before scaling

**Next: research, prototype, dry run, pilot.**

1. Survey graduates and interview employers to select a computer-based field.
2. Create one fictional business case, rubric, and facilitator guide.
3. Test timing, access, and scoring with a dry run.
4. Prepare consent, privacy, moderation, and solution-use terms.
5. Run one small pilot and publish what the evidence shows.

**Limits today:** no product, research, pilot, employer partner, bounty, or placement outcome is confirmed. The format can add evidence; it cannot guarantee a job or solve unemployment on its own.
